# PU3NTE Skool Module: Spanish Verb Conjugation Bootcamp

Use this as the structure for the Skool module. Each tense gets two pages:

1. A main explanation page for the normal pattern.
2. A separate irregulars/exceptions page for the same tense.

The learning flow is: understand the job of the tense → learn the regular pattern → notice the most common exceptions → practise actively in the PU3NTE conjugator → use the forms in sentences.

This module teaches Spanish verbs as a usable speaking system, not as a list to memorise. The goal is not to “know grammar” in theory. The goal is to recognise what a tense is doing, build the form quickly, and use it in real sentences.

Each tense is split into two pages. First, you learn the normal pattern: what the tense means, when to use it, and how regular -ar, -er, and -ir verbs work. Then you study the irregulars separately, because Spanish irregulars are easier when you see them as families: stem changes, spelling changes, yo-go verbs, irregular past stems, irregular future stems, irregular participles, and command exceptions.

# 1. Present Tense

## Page 1A: Present Tense — Regular Forms

### Skool Page Title
Present Tense: How to Talk About Now, Habits, and General Truths

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-present-tense-practice

### Copy
The Spanish present tense is your everyday tense. Use it for things happening now, habits, routines, general truths, and near-future plans when the context is clear.

Examples:

- Hablo español todos los días. = I speak Spanish every day.
- Trabajo en casa. = I work at home.
- Mañana salimos temprano. = Tomorrow we leave early.

Spanish regular verbs fall into three families: -ar, -er, and -ir.

For -ar verbs like hablar, remove -ar and add:

- yo hablo
- tú hablas
- él / ella / usted habla
- nosotros hablamos
- vosotros habláis
- ellos / ellas / ustedes hablan

For -er verbs like comer:

- yo como
- tú comes
- él / ella / usted come
- nosotros comemos
- vosotros coméis
- ellos / ellas / ustedes comen

For -ir verbs like vivir:

- yo vivo
- tú vives
- él / ella / usted vive
- nosotros vivimos
- vosotros vivís
- ellos / ellas / ustedes viven

The fastest way to learn the present tense is to stop thinking of it as six separate words. Learn the sound pattern:

- -ar: o, as, a, amos, áis, an
- -er: o, es, e, emos, éis, en
- -ir: o, es, e, imos, ís, en

When practising, say the subject and verb together: yo hablo, tú hablas, ella habla. This builds automatic speech rhythm.

## Page 1B: Present Tense — Irregulars and Exceptions

### Skool Page Title
Present Tense Irregulars: Stem Changes, Yo-Go Verbs, and Core Exceptions

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-present-tense-practice

### Copy
Present tense irregulars are common, but they are not random. Most fall into patterns.

First pattern: stem-changing verbs. The vowel changes in most forms, but usually not nosotros or vosotros.

Examples:

- pensar → pienso, piensas, piensa, pensamos, pensáis, piensan
- dormir → duermo, duermes, duerme, dormimos, dormís, duermen
- pedir → pido, pides, pide, pedimos, pedís, piden

Second pattern: yo-go verbs. The yo form ends in -go, but many other forms are regular or stem-changing.

Examples:

- tener → tengo
- hacer → hago
- decir → digo
- salir → salgo
- poner → pongo
- venir → vengo
- traer → traigo
- oír → oigo

Third pattern: high-frequency full irregulars. These must be learned as chunks because they are used constantly.

- ser → soy, eres, es, somos, sois, son
- estar → estoy, estás, está, estamos, estáis, están
- ir → voy, vas, va, vamos, vais, van
- dar → doy, das, da, damos, dais, dan
- saber → sé, sabes, sabe, sabemos, sabéis, saben

Do not try to memorise every irregular verb in one sitting. Learn the most useful verbs first, then sort new verbs into families.

# 2. Preterite

## Page 2A: Preterite — Regular Forms

### Skool Page Title
Preterite: Completed Actions in the Past

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-preterite-tense-practice

### Copy
Use the preterite for completed actions in the past. It answers: What happened? What did someone do? What event moved the story forward?

Examples:

- Ayer hablé con Ana. = Yesterday I spoke with Ana.
- Compramos comida. = We bought food.
- Vivieron en México. = They lived in Mexico.

Regular -ar endings:

- yo hablé
- tú hablaste
- él / ella / usted habló
- nosotros hablamos
- vosotros hablasteis
- ellos / ellas / ustedes hablaron

Regular -er and -ir endings:

- yo comí / viví
- tú comiste / viviste
- él comió / vivió
- nosotros comimos / vivimos
- vosotros comisteis / vivisteis
- ellos comieron / vivieron

Notice that nosotros forms for regular -ar and -ir verbs can look the same as present: hablamos, vivimos. Context tells you if it means present or past.

Use the preterite when the action is seen as finished:

- Llegué tarde. = I arrived late.
- Le escribí ayer. = I wrote to him/her yesterday.
- Terminé el trabajo. = I finished the work.

## Page 2B: Preterite — Irregulars and Exceptions

### Skool Page Title
Preterite Irregulars: Strong Stems, Spelling Changes, and Y-Changes

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-preterite-tense-practice

### Copy
The preterite has several important exception families.

Spelling-change verbs keep the same sound:

- buscar → busqué
- pagar → pagué
- empezar → empecé
- jugar → jugué

Y-change verbs add y in the third person because of the vowel contact:

- leer → leyó, leyeron
- oír → oyó, oyeron
- caer → cayó, cayeron

Strong-stem irregulars use a new stem and usually do not carry written accents:

- tener → tuve, tuviste, tuvo, tuvimos, tuvisteis, tuvieron
- estar → estuve, estuviste, estuvo...
- poder → pude, pudiste, pudo...
- poner → puse, pusiste, puso...
- saber → supe, supiste, supo...
- venir → vine, viniste, vino...

Some verbs are very irregular:

- ser / ir → fui, fuiste, fue, fuimos, fuisteis, fueron
- hacer → hice, hiciste, hizo, hicimos, hicisteis, hicieron
- decir → dije, dijiste, dijo, dijimos, dijisteis, dijeron
- traer → traje, trajiste, trajo, trajimos, trajisteis, trajeron

Important: do not add an extra -s to tú forms. It is dijiste, not dijistes.

# 3. Imperfect

## Page 3A: Imperfect — Regular Forms

### Skool Page Title
Imperfect: Background, Habits, and “Used To”

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-imperfect-tense-practice

### Copy
Use the imperfect for background information, repeated past actions, descriptions, age, time, feelings, and what used to happen.

Examples:

- Cuando era niño, jugaba mucho. = When I was a kid, I used to play a lot.
- Vivíamos cerca del centro. = We lived near downtown.
- Estaba cansado. = I was tired.

Regular -ar endings:

- yo hablaba
- tú hablabas
- él / ella / usted hablaba
- nosotros hablábamos
- vosotros hablabais
- ellos / ellas / ustedes hablaban

Regular -er and -ir endings:

- yo comía / vivía
- tú comías / vivías
- él comía / vivía
- nosotros comíamos / vivíamos
- vosotros comíais / vivíais
- ellos comían / vivían

Think of the imperfect as the “camera” tense. It sets the scene. The preterite says what happened; the imperfect describes what was going on.

Compare:

- Caminaba por la calle cuando me llamó. = I was walking down the street when he/she called me.
- Caminé a la tienda. = I walked to the store.

## Page 3B: Imperfect — Irregulars and Exceptions

### Skool Page Title
Imperfect Irregulars: The Three You Must Know

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-imperfect-tense-practice

### Copy
The imperfect is one of the friendliest Spanish tenses because it has only three major irregular verbs.

Ser:

- era, eras, era, éramos, erais, eran

Ir:

- iba, ibas, iba, íbamos, ibais, iban

Ver:

- veía, veías, veía, veíamos, veíais, veían

That is the core. Most other verbs, including many verbs that are irregular elsewhere, behave regularly in the imperfect:

- tener → tenía
- hacer → hacía
- decir → decía
- venir → venía
- poder → podía

The main challenge is not the form. The challenge is choosing imperfect vs preterite. Ask yourself: Am I describing the scene/habit/background, or am I reporting a completed event?

# 4. Future

## Page 4A: Future — Regular Forms

### Skool Page Title
Future Tense: What Will Happen

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-future-tense-practice

### Copy
The future tense is used for what will happen, promises, predictions, and sometimes guesses.

Regular future forms are simple because you keep the whole infinitive and add the ending:

- hablaré, hablarás, hablará, hablaremos, hablaréis, hablarán
- comeré, comerás, comerá, comeremos, comeréis, comerán
- viviré, vivirás, vivirá, viviremos, viviréis, vivirán

The endings are the same for -ar, -er, and -ir:

- é, ás, á, emos, éis, án

Examples:

- Mañana hablaré con él. = Tomorrow I will speak with him.
- Comeremos después. = We will eat later.
- Vivirán en otra ciudad. = They will live in another city.

In everyday speech, Spanish often uses ir a + infinitive instead of the future:

- Voy a estudiar. = I’m going to study.

But the future tense is still important for commitments, predictions, formal plans, and educated speech.

## Page 4B: Future — Irregulars and Exceptions

### Skool Page Title
Future Irregulars: Same Endings, New Stem

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-future-tense-practice

### Copy
Future irregulars are easier than they look. The endings stay the same. Only the stem changes.

Common irregular stems:

- tener → tendr- → tendré
- poner → pondr- → pondré
- salir → saldr- → saldré
- venir → vendr- → vendré
- decir → dir- → diré
- hacer → har- → haré
- poder → podr- → podré
- querer → querr- → querré
- saber → sabr- → sabré

Once you know the stem, add the normal endings:

- tendré, tendrás, tendrá, tendremos, tendréis, tendrán
- haré, harás, hará, haremos, haréis, harán
- diré, dirás, dirá, diremos, diréis, dirán

Do not memorise these as separate full tables. Memorise the irregular stem family and then attach the regular future endings.

# 5. Conditional

## Page 5A: Conditional — Regular Forms

### Skool Page Title
Conditional: Would, Could, and Polite Requests

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-conditional-tense-practice

### Copy
The conditional is the “would” tense. Use it for hypothetical situations, polite requests, suggestions, and soft opinions.

Examples:

- Hablaría contigo, pero no tengo tiempo. = I would speak with you, but I don’t have time.
- ¿Podrías ayudarme? = Could you help me?
- Sería mejor esperar. = It would be better to wait.

Like the future, regular conditional forms keep the infinitive and add endings:

- hablaría, hablarías, hablaría, hablaríamos, hablaríais, hablarían
- comería, comerías, comería, comeríamos, comeríais, comerían
- viviría, vivirías, viviría, viviríamos, viviríais, vivirían

The endings are:

- ía, ías, ía, íamos, íais, ían

The conditional is extremely useful for sounding softer and more adult in Spanish.

## Page 5B: Conditional — Irregulars and Exceptions

### Skool Page Title
Conditional Irregulars: Future Stems Reused

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-conditional-tense-practice

### Copy
The conditional uses the same irregular stems as the future.

Examples:

- tener → tendría
- poner → pondría
- salir → saldría
- venir → vendría
- decir → diría
- hacer → haría
- poder → podría
- querer → querría
- saber → sabría

This is good news. If you learn the future irregular stems, you already know the conditional irregular stems.

Examples in real phrases:

- Tendría que pensarlo. = I would have to think about it.
- Podríamos hacerlo mañana. = We could do it tomorrow.
- Yo no diría eso. = I wouldn’t say that.
- Sería mejor hablar claro. = It would be better to speak clearly.

The biggest mistake is using conditional after si in the if-clause. Say:

- Si tuviera tiempo, iría. = If I had time, I would go.

Not: Si tendría tiempo...

# 6. Present Perfect

## Page 6A: Present Perfect — Regular Forms

### Skool Page Title
Present Perfect: What Has Happened

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-present-perfect-practice

### Copy
The present perfect uses haber in the present plus the past participle.

Pattern:

- he hablado
- has hablado
- ha hablado
- hemos hablado
- habéis hablado
- han hablado

Regular participles:

- -ar → -ado: hablar → hablado
- -er / -ir → -ido: comer → comido, vivir → vivido

Examples:

- He estudiado mucho. = I have studied a lot.
- Hemos comido. = We have eaten.
- ¿Has vivido aquí antes? = Have you lived here before?

Use it for experiences and actions connected to the present:

- He aprendido mucho. = I have learned a lot.
- No he terminado todavía. = I haven’t finished yet.

In Latin America, the simple preterite is often more common than the present perfect for recent finished events, but the present perfect is still essential for experiences, “already / not yet”, and formal Spanish.

## Page 6B: Present Perfect — Irregulars and Exceptions

### Skool Page Title
Present Perfect Irregulars: Irregular Participles

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-present-perfect-practice

### Copy
The present perfect irregulars come from the participle, not from haber. Haber stays regular:

- he, has, ha, hemos, habéis, han

The participle is the part that changes.

Common irregular participles:

- hacer → hecho
- decir → dicho
- ver → visto
- poner → puesto
- volver → vuelto
- abrir → abierto
- escribir → escrito
- romper → roto
- morir → muerto
- cubrir → cubierto

Examples:

- He hecho la tarea. = I have done the homework.
- Hemos visto esa película. = We have seen that movie.
- Ya han abierto la puerta. = They have already opened the door.
- No has dicho nada. = You haven’t said anything.

Important rule: in these perfect tenses, the participle does not change for gender or number.

- He visto la casa.
- He visto los mensajes.

It stays visto because it belongs to haber.

# 7. Past Perfect

## Page 7A: Past Perfect — Regular Forms

### Skool Page Title
Past Perfect: What Had Happened Before Something Else

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-past-perfect-practice

### Copy
The past perfect is used for an action that had already happened before another past moment.

Pattern:

- había hablado
- habías hablado
- había hablado
- habíamos hablado
- habíais hablado
- habían hablado

Examples:

- Cuando llegué, ella ya había salido. = When I arrived, she had already left.
- No entendí porque no había estudiado. = I didn’t understand because I hadn’t studied.
- Habíamos comido antes de la reunión. = We had eaten before the meeting.

Think of it as the “past before the past” tense.

Timeline:

1. First: ella salió.
2. Later: yo llegué.
3. Sentence: Cuando llegué, ella ya había salido.

Use it when you need to show that one past action happened earlier than another.

## Page 7B: Past Perfect — Irregulars and Exceptions

### Skool Page Title
Past Perfect Irregulars: Same Participles, Past Haber

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-past-perfect-practice

### Copy
Past perfect irregulars work exactly like present perfect irregulars. Haber changes to the imperfect, and the participle may be irregular.

Haber:

- había, habías, había, habíamos, habíais, habían

Irregular participles:

- hacer → hecho
- decir → dicho
- ver → visto
- poner → puesto
- volver → vuelto
- abrir → abierto
- escribir → escrito
- romper → roto
- morir → muerto
- cubrir → cubierto

Examples:

- Yo había hecho todo. = I had done everything.
- Ella había dicho la verdad. = She had told the truth.
- Nosotros habíamos visto el problema. = We had seen the problem.
- Ellos habían puesto la mesa. = They had set the table.

Again, the participle does not change with gender or number when used with haber.

# 8. Present Subjunctive

## Page 8A: Present Subjunctive — Regular Forms

### Skool Page Title
Present Subjunctive: Wants, Doubts, Feelings, and Uncertainty

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-present-subjunctive-practice

### Copy
The present subjunctive is not just a tense; it is a mood. It usually appears after another phrase that expresses desire, doubt, emotion, necessity, possibility, or uncertainty.

Common triggers:

- Quiero que...
- Espero que...
- Es importante que...
- Dudo que...
- No creo que...
- Antes de que...
- Para que...

Regular formation:

1. Start with the yo form of the present.
2. Remove the -o.
3. Add the opposite vowel endings.

For -ar verbs, use e endings:

- hable, hables, hable, hablemos, habléis, hablen

For -er and -ir verbs, use a endings:

- coma, comas, coma, comamos, comáis, coman
- viva, vivas, viva, vivamos, viváis, vivan

Examples:

- Quiero que hables con ella. = I want you to speak with her.
- Es importante que comamos bien. = It is important that we eat well.
- Espero que vivan cerca. = I hope they live nearby.

## Page 8B: Present Subjunctive — Irregulars and Exceptions

### Skool Page Title
Present Subjunctive Irregulars: Yo-Form Logic and Six Core Exceptions

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-present-subjunctive-practice

### Copy
Many present subjunctive irregulars come from the present tense yo form.

Examples:

- tener → tengo → tenga
- poner → pongo → ponga
- salir → salgo → salga
- hacer → hago → haga
- decir → digo → diga
- venir → vengo → venga
- traer → traigo → traiga
- oír → oigo → oiga

Spelling changes protect pronunciation:

- buscar → busque
- pagar → pague
- empezar → empiece / empecemos
- jugar → juegue / juguemos

Stem-changing verbs often keep their stem changes:

- pensar → piense, pienses, piense, pensemos, penséis, piensen
- dormir → duerma, duermas, duerma, durmamos, durmáis, duerman
- pedir → pida, pidas, pida, pidamos, pidáis, pidan

Six high-frequency verbs are fully irregular:

- ser → sea
- estar → esté
- ir → vaya
- dar → dé
- saber → sepa
- haber → haya

Learn these as chunks inside real trigger phrases:

- Quiero que vayas.
- Espero que estés bien.
- No creo que sea fácil.
- Es mejor que lo hagas.

# 9. Imperfect Subjunctive

## Page 9A: Imperfect Subjunctive — Regular Forms

### Skool Page Title
Imperfect Subjunctive: Hypotheticals, Wishes, and Reported Wants

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-imperfect-subjunctive-practice

### Copy
The imperfect subjunctive is used for hypothetical situations, wishes, polite requests, and past-tense subjunctive situations.

Common patterns:

- Si tuviera tiempo, iría. = If I had time, I would go.
- Quería que vinieras. = I wanted you to come.
- Ojalá fuera más fácil. = If only it were easier.
- Me gustaría que me ayudaras. = I would like you to help me.

How to form it:

1. Take the ellos/ellas/ustedes preterite form.
2. Remove -ron.
3. Add: -ra, -ras, -ra, -ramos, -rais, -ran.

Example with hablar:

- hablaron → habla-
- hablara, hablaras, hablara, habláramos, hablarais, hablaran

Example with comer:

- comieron → comie-
- comiera, comieras, comiera, comiéramos, comierais, comieran

The nosotros form carries an accent: habláramos, comiéramos, viviéramos.

## Page 9B: Imperfect Subjunctive — Irregulars and Exceptions

### Skool Page Title
Imperfect Subjunctive Irregulars: The Preterite Stem Is the Key

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-imperfect-subjunctive-practice

### Copy
The imperfect subjunctive feels irregular, but it follows the preterite. If the ellos preterite is irregular, the imperfect subjunctive uses that irregular stem.

Examples:

- tener → tuvieron → tuviera
- estar → estuvieron → estuviera
- poder → pudieron → pudiera
- poner → pusieron → pusiera
- saber → supieron → supiera
- venir → vinieron → viniera
- hacer → hicieron → hiciera
- decir → dijeron → dijera
- traer → trajeron → trajera
- ser / ir → fueron → fuera

The -se forms are also correct:

- tuviera / tuviese
- fuera / fuese
- hiciera / hiciese
- dijera / dijese

The PU3NTE tool accepts the -se alternatives, but the main form taught here is -ra because it is very common across the Spanish-speaking world.

Use it in full phrases:

- Si tuviera más tiempo, practicaría más.
- Quería que me dijeras la verdad.
- Me gustaría que fueras directo.

# 10. Imperative

## Page 10A: Imperative — Regular Forms

### Skool Page Title
Imperative: Commands, Instructions, and Friendly Direction

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-imperative-practice

### Copy
The imperative is used for commands, instructions, invitations, and direct suggestions.

Examples:

- Habla conmigo. = Talk to me.
- Come algo. = Eat something.
- Vive tranquilo. = Live calmly.

Regular affirmative commands:

For tú:

- hablar → habla
- comer → come
- vivir → vive

For usted:

- hable
- coma
- viva

For nosotros:

- hablemos
- comamos
- vivamos

For vosotros:

- hablad
- comed
- vivid

For ustedes:

- hablen
- coman
- vivan

The imperative is not always rude. Tone matters. With por favor, softeners, and context, it can sound natural and friendly:

- Espera un momento, por favor.
- Pásame eso, si puedes.
- Hablemos mañana.

## Page 10B: Imperative — Irregulars and Exceptions

### Skool Page Title
Imperative Irregulars: Short Commands You Must Know

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-imperative-practice

### Copy
The most important irregular affirmative tú commands are short and very common:

- decir → di
- hacer → haz
- ir → ve
- poner → pon
- salir → sal
- ser → sé
- tener → ten
- venir → ven

Examples:

- Di la verdad. = Tell the truth.
- Hazlo ahora. = Do it now.
- Ve con cuidado. = Go carefully.
- Ponlo aquí. = Put it here.
- Ten paciencia. = Be patient.

For usted and ustedes commands, Spanish normally uses the present subjunctive:

- haga / hagan
- diga / digan
- vaya / vayan
- tenga / tengan
- ponga / pongan

Negative commands also use the subjunctive:

- No vengas.
- No lo hagas.
- No digas eso.

The current PU3NTE imperative lab focuses on affirmative command forms.

# 11. Gerund

## Page 11A: Gerund — Regular Forms

### Skool Page Title
Gerund: The Spanish -ing Form

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-gerund-practice

### Copy
The gerund is the Spanish equivalent of the -ing form when talking about an action in progress.

Regular formation:

- -ar → -ando: hablar → hablando
- -er / -ir → -iendo: comer → comiendo, vivir → viviendo

Most common structure:

- estar + gerund

Examples:

- Estoy hablando. = I am speaking.
- Está comiendo. = He/she is eating.
- Estamos viviendo aquí. = We are living here.

The gerund does not change for person:

- yo estoy hablando
- tú estás hablando
- ella está hablando
- nosotros estamos hablando

The form hablando stays the same.

## Page 11B: Gerund — Irregulars and Exceptions

### Skool Page Title
Gerund Irregulars: Yendo, Diciendo, Pidiendo, Durmiendo

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-gerund-practice

### Copy
Gerund irregulars are common but patterned.

When -iendo creates awkward vowel contact, Spanish often uses y:

- ir → yendo
- leer → leyendo
- oír → oyendo
- traer → trayendo

Some -ir stem-changing verbs change inside the gerund:

- decir → diciendo
- pedir → pidiendo
- dormir → durmiendo
- venir → viniendo
- morir → muriendo

Examples:

- Estoy yendo al trabajo. = I am going to work.
- Está diciendo la verdad. = He/she is telling the truth.
- Estamos pidiendo comida. = We are ordering food.
- Se está durmiendo. = He/she is falling asleep.

Remember: the gerund is a form, not a full tense by itself. It usually needs another verb, especially estar.

# 12. Past Participle

## Page 12A: Past Participle — Regular Forms

### Skool Page Title
Past Participle: The Form Used with Haber

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-past-participle-practice

### Copy
The past participle is used in perfect tenses with haber.

Regular formation:

- -ar → -ado: hablar → hablado
- -er / -ir → -ido: comer → comido, vivir → vivido

Examples:

- He hablado. = I have spoken.
- Habíamos comido. = We had eaten.
- Han vivido aquí. = They have lived here.

With haber, the participle does not change:

- He comprado una casa.
- He comprado dos casas.

Comprado stays comprado because it is part of the verb phrase with haber.

The participle can also work like an adjective, and then it does change:

- La puerta está abierta.
- Los libros están abiertos.

That is a separate use.

## Page 12B: Past Participle — Irregulars and Exceptions

### Skool Page Title
Past Participle Irregulars: Hecho, Dicho, Visto, Puesto, and More

### Practice Link
https://pu3nte-static.vercel.app/verbs/spanish-past-participle-practice

### Copy
Irregular participles are extremely important because they appear in present perfect, past perfect, passive structures, and adjectives.

Common irregular participles:

- hacer → hecho
- decir → dicho
- ver → visto
- poner → puesto
- volver → vuelto
- abrir → abierto
- escribir → escrito
- romper → roto
- morir → muerto
- cubrir → cubierto

Examples with haber:

- He hecho mucho. = I have done a lot.
- Habíamos visto el problema. = We had seen the problem.
- Ella ha escrito un mensaje. = She has written a message.
- Ya habían abierto. = They had already opened.

Examples as adjectives:

- La puerta está abierta.
- El vaso está roto.
- La comida está hecha.

Be careful: with haber, the participle stays fixed. As an adjective, it agrees with the noun.
